## Gotchas / decisions made
- The identified servo model (P only) has a 6.5 Hz, zeta ~0.4 resonance; the runtime injects joint damping (`q_cmd -= 0.03 * speed`) and an upper-body integrator as stand-ins for the Feetech D and I registers. Untethered, servo sag (3-6 deg pelvis pitch) needs integral action: the engine's tether hid it.
- Feeding the lagged raw COM measurement back into the footstep rule is unstable (tested many ways); measured data enters through a touchdown re-seed (30 %) and a slow plan-error bias. Gains were tuned on one seed, nominal mass/friction: **not validated under randomisation**.
- The standing **capture step is implemented but opt-in (`capture_enable=False`)**: measured to be no better than, and between 0.8 and 1.0 N s worse than, the passive servo stiffness + IMU ankle loop. Standing capacity is ~0.8-1.0 N s fwd/back, 1.3 N s sideways; walking 0.94 / 0.81 / 0.50 (left, toward the outside of the stance foot) / 0.88 N s against the provisional 0.45 N s requirement.
- 100 Hz may not fit one TTL bus with 23 servos (estimate ~9-10 ms of traffic); at 50 Hz the runtime stands, dances and walks in sim but the 2 m walk stops at 1.75 m (-12 %).
- Pi 5 compute is an ESTIMATE (host x3.5), not measured.
- Scratch experiments are in `runs/` (git-ignored).
- Candidate lesson row for `coordination/LESSONS.md` (not edited, outside this task's paths): "The concept engine's push plan assumed instant unloading of the stepping foot and the engine replays with a 6 % tether; closed-loop untethered in sim the standing capture step did not extend recovery and a controller that fed back the lagged COM measurement was unstable. Rule: validate stepping/feedback claims in the closed-loop runtime (`openhumanoid.runtime`) before they enter a requirement or a gate."
